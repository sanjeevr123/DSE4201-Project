\begin{center}\textbf{ABSTRACT}\end{center}
Stock--bond correlation turned positive in 2022, which raises the question of whether tail-risk forecasts improve when the correlation is allowed to switch with a market regime. I forecast one-day value-at-risk and expected shortfall for an equal-weight portfolio of SPY, IEF and GLD with a ladder of models that vary the treatment of volatility, correlation and the mean under one two-state regime signal, and score them with the FZ0 loss. Before touching 2020--2025, I fixed one confirmatory test, switching the SPY--IEF correlation against constant correlation at the 5\% tail level, together with a minimum detectable difference, a smallest effect of interest and a simulated power study. On the 1,508 hold-out days the switching model's loss was lower by 0.0013 (95\% interval $-0.0037$ to $+0.0012$; one-sided $p=0.158$). The test did not reject. It could detect a difference of 0.0031 with 80\% probability, and its simulated power was 18\% to 35\% for the shifts I simulated, so the data are consistent with a gain and with none. Both primary models breached the 5\% VaR on about 6.9\% of hold-out days and breach-day losses exceeded the forecast expected shortfall. On the development data, regime-specific volatility lowered the loss in three of four ETF samples, and correlation steps were small against their detectable differences. The thesis reports what a sample of this length can and cannot detect, and does not claim that regime-switching correlation is absent or present.
