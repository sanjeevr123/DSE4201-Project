\begin{center}\textbf{ABSTRACT}\end{center}
When the stock--bond correlation turned positive in 2022, portfolios of equities and Treasuries lost part of their hedge. I ask whether a risk manager forecasting one-day value-at-risk (VaR) and expected shortfall (ES) for an equal-weight portfolio of SPY, IEF and GLD should let the SPY--IEF correlation switch with a two-state market regime. The switching model and a constant-correlation model share the same GARCH margins and are scored with the FZ0 joint loss. The test, its decision rule, a smallest effect of interest and its simulated power were fixed before the 2020--2025 hold-out was opened, and the test was run once. Over 1,508 hold-out days, switching lowered the average loss by 0.0013 (95\% interval $-0.0037$ to $+0.0012$; one-sided $p=0.158$). The test could detect only differences above 0.0031, so a useful gain is neither shown nor ruled out, and the favourable part of the difference came in 2021 rather than in the 2022 reversal. A larger problem is shared by both models: each breached its 5\% VaR on about 6.8\% to 6.9\% of days, and breach-day losses exceeded the forecast ES. A simple EWMA, scored post hoc, breached on 5.2\% of days with a loss that could not be distinguished from either model's. On development data, regime information helped through volatility rather than correlation. For this portfolio the evidence gives no reason to adopt regime-switching correlation, and the scale of the forecast matters more than the correlation model.
