\begin{center}\textbf{ABSTRACT}\end{center}
Stock--bond correlation turned positive in 2022, which raises the question of whether tail-risk forecasts improve when the correlation is allowed to switch with a market regime. I forecast one-day value-at-risk and expected shortfall for an equal-weight portfolio of SPY, IEF and GLD and compare two models with the same GARCH margins: constant SPY--IEF correlation, and a correlation that switches with a two-state hidden Markov regime fitted to SPY. Forecasts are scored with the FZ0 loss. The comparison, its 5\% tail level, its decision rule, a smallest effect of interest and a simulated power study were fixed before the 2020--2025 hold-out was used. On the 1,508 hold-out days the switching model's loss was lower by 0.0013 (95\% interval $-0.0037$ to $+0.0012$; one-sided $p=0.158$). The test did not reject. It could detect a difference of 0.0031 with 80\% probability, and an improvement equal to the smallest effect of interest, 0.0020, would have been detected with probability about 48\%, so the data are consistent with a gain and with none. Both models breached the 5\% VaR on about 6.8\% to 6.9\% of hold-out days, and breach-day losses exceeded the forecast expected shortfall; a post hoc EWMA baseline breached on 5.2\% of days, with a loss 0.0063 above that of constant correlation (standard error 0.0440). On the development data, regime-specific volatility lowered the loss against a pooled normal in three of four ETF samples, but GARCH margins did better still, and correlation steps were small against their detectable differences. The thesis reports what a sample of this length can and cannot detect, and does not claim that regime-switching correlation is absent or present.
