\begin{center}\textbf{ACKNOWLEDGEMENTS}\end{center}

This thesis is about assets that usually steady one another and what happens when they stop doing so under stress. The people named here did the opposite: they held steady throughout.

My first thanks go to my supervisor, Associate Professor Juwon Seo of the Department of Economics, for taking this project on and guiding it from a broad first question to a single, carefully posed test. The feedback I received was candid and always constructive, and the insistence on never claiming more than a comparison can show, and on keeping ``no significant improvement'' apart from ``no improvement'', shaped how every result in these pages is reported. It has been a privilege to learn econometrics under such supervision.
